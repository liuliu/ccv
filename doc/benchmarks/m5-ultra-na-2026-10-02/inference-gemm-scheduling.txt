INT8 inference GEMM: inner-loop scheduling, 2026-10-03

Machine: Apple M5 Ultra, 80 GPU cores, 256 GB, macOS 27.0.1.
MLX checkout: 25532871329fe4cafdada9fa0941a1cdbfb3a9f2, including PR #4447
(2d27ab05fb7dcda69bb3c57abd74c0b3bc9a5a99). The MLX comparison binary includes
only the previously documented D256 attention JIT-signature fix; its GEMM
implementation and tuning are unchanged.

The retained change unrolls two successive K32 fragment iterations in the INT8
register GEMM. It exposes more loads and multiplies to the compiler while
retaining the 512-element outer reduction and its threadgroup barrier, the
compiler barrier, exact INT32 accumulation, rowwise scales and optional bias.
It does not change the output tile, activation quantization, weight format,
shape selector, or the existing centralized hardware profile. The FP16 shader
source is unchanged. The imported MLX file is also unchanged: only its copy
used to generate the INT8 shader receives the pragma replacement.

This improves FFN-down reductions across all priority model families. Q/up
projections generally change by less than 1%, and remain a gap versus 2x MLX.
No claim is made that these measurements isolate physical GPU utilization or
prove a specific instruction-latency bottleneck.

The initial seven-shape pilot compares six scheduling choices in reversed
orders: 84 successful executions. Removing the compiler barrier, unrolling
four iterations, and combinations of both offer mixed results. Unrolling two
while preserving that barrier is the general choice. The following 83-case
sweep compares previous INT8, unroll-two INT8 and actual local MLX FP16 in two
reversed orders: 498 successful executions. It preserves the 81 distinct shapes
and fixed/dynamic M flags in inference-int8-register-workloads.json. All 166
previous/new sampled CPU-error pairs are identical.

Four extra reversed-order passes on eight cases add 64 successful executions,
checking noisy unchanged fallbacks, the weakest selected cases and priority
projections. The repeat confirms about a 3.3% regression at M8193/N1537/K6145,
with partial N and K tiles. Other selected losses are below 1% in the combined
medians. The 6-13% priority FFN-down gains justify this small boundary tradeoff;
no additional alignment, model-name or chip-name gate is added. The anomalous
speedups on unchanged fallback kernels are timing variation and are not claimed
as implementation gains. All raw samples, including those outliers, remain.

Production verification uses a fresh archive and freshly linked probes. The
13-case comparison includes the exact UI H3 Q/up/down shapes at 480p/10s and
720p/10s, four image FFN-down shapes and three boundary controls. There are 78
successful records: three implementations in two reversed orders, three warmup
iterations plus at least 0.5 GPU seconds, and nine timed samples per process.
The table is the median of the two process GPU medians, in milliseconds.
Activation quantization is included; weights are already rowwise INT8. MLX
executes its ordinary FP16 matmul backend and all unevaluated GPU preparation.
These are individual operator times, not full image/video generation times.

Numerical checks include 64 independent executions on eight shapes, both
fixed/dynamic M and both FP16/INT8 arithmetic. Every one of the 32 previous/new
full-output SHA256 pairs is identical, including K5376, K14336, K32768 and
K32769, partial tiles, and selector fallbacks. All values are finite. The
existing twelve-shape randomized integration test also passes: sampled full
rows are compared with an independently quantized CPU GEMM, with bias, zero
rows, changing cache shapes, partial M/N/K and selection boundaries. Its
relative-L2 bound is 5e-4 against quantized inputs, with absolute bound 3e-3.
Those checks establish equivalence to the prior quantized implementation;
they do not establish INT8 model quality against unquantized weights.

Full production validation passes: MPSBLAS 298/298 and MPSDNN 126/126, both
exit zero. The library and both test binaries were rebuilt/relinked before
execution. All CPU compilation finished before GPU timing. All GPU workloads
were serial with unrestricted Metal access. git diff --check passes.

An isolated D128 attention dispatch pilot accompanies this checkpoint. It
compares the existing Morton order with compact row-major, head-major, and
eight-query-group chunks on nine shapes in reversed orders: 72 executions.
All full-output hashes match the baseline. It uses dynamic R and C, which
differ from the final application timing protocol; its H3 medians must not
replace the application table in inference-output-split.txt. Its compact-order
gains are experimental until application-path and broader shape checks pass.
No D128 scheduling change is included in this production checkpoint.

No attention layout conversion is introduced. Backward tuning remains
deferred; prior MLX VJP comparisons are preserved. The remaining inference
gaps are H3 Q/up and D128 image attention. Investigations should target their
reduction/fragment scheduling and per-tile state costs. Further shape gates
need evidence, and the rejected staging/register prototypes remain rejected.

Files prefixed inference-gemm-scheduling contain raw CSVs, exact shapes/flags,
all medians, numerical hashes, validation outcomes, build/raw logs, source
snapshots, the integration diff and SHA256 provenance. The D128 experiment is
archived separately under inference-d128-grid. Prototype environment controls
are restricted to scratch binaries; production has no experimental switches.
All source changes remain unstaged and uncommitted.

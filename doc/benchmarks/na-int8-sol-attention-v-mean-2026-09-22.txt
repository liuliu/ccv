Single-pass Sol V-mean feasibility probes, Apple M5 Max, 2026-09-22.
Reduction-only GPU command-buffer timing; these are NOT end-to-end attention timings.
FP16 [N,T,H,128] input, FP32 per-head/channel means, uniform random seed42 [-3,3].
Four warmup rounds and sixteen measured rounds; reverse variant order on alternating rounds.
Compare the existing coalesced two-pass reduction, a native-style single-pass linear grid,
a native-style Morton grid, and single-pass tiled reductions using threadgroup memory.
All candidates preserve the current per-thread accumulation and XOR-shuffle reduction order.
Long-sequence tiled variants cover 16, 32, or 64 channels per threadgroup (tile4/tile8/tile16 float4 vectors).
Short-sequence tile8 and tile16 variants use 16 and 32 channels to fit threadgroup memory.
The wide variant reuses its partials buffer for the final reduction to stay within 32 KiB.
All reported medians are over sixteen measured rounds. TILED/WIDE report medians only;
the per-round lines report the two-pass, linear-grid, and Morton-grid timings.

Result: one launch is correct, but the best single-pass candidate was slower at all three
realistic long shapes. Production remains two-pass. Native-style Morton was 2.9-3.8x slower;
the best tiled single-pass version was 3.3%, 7.9%, and 11.6% slower at T32768/65536/103982.
At T257/H2/N2, the simple single-pass versions were slightly faster (about 0.0005 ms saved).
These results do not establish behavior for other devices or unmeasured head counts.

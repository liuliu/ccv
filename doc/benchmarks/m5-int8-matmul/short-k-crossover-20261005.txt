Max register vs native64: the two M/N-tail cases improve 12.9% and 9.9%.
The two losing aligned cases regress 1.2% and 3.5%; the other three cases
change by less than 1%. For these shapes, 10-core production still selects native128.

Crossover estimates from both measured devices:
Let D(C)=Tregister(C)-Tnative(C). Two fits to D(10),D(40):
  linear gap: C=10+30*D(10)/(D(10)-D(40))
  inverse-core gap A+B/C: C=40*(D(10)-D(40))/(D(10)-4*D(40))
Against native64, the two losing aligned shapes project ties at 40.4-40.8
(linear) or 41.6-43.3 (inverse-core) cores. These are extrapolations, since
neither measured endpoint favors register. Tail cases favor register over
native64 at BOTH endpoints: there is no bracketed crossing for that pair.
Against native128, the M/N-tail crossings are 25.9-27.7 (linear) or 16.6-17.9
(inverse-core). The retained 36-core gate is a conservative policy choice,
not a newly measured universal crossover. No 36-core or Ultra GPU was tested.

The estimates depend on shape, model, clocks, bandwidth, and cache as well as
core count. Exact proportional T=B/C scaling cannot create a crossover.
iPad launch variation was substantial, particularly with native64; all runs

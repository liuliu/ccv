AttentionR1 decode validation

The forward decode path supports FP16/BF16, R1/R2 and D128/D256. Its kernel
and selector are described in implementation.txt and gating-review.txt.

MLX uses stride views of the same B/S/H/D input storage. Its multi-batch
vector attention performs internal contiguous copies inside the timing;
comparisons measure complete operations. CCV adds no physical transpose.
A material slowdown requires both >5 percent and >2 microseconds.
The repeatable MLX gap is R2/C32768/B1/H16:8/D128: 139.333 vs 130.730 us
(6.58 percent). An intermittent R2/C2049/B2/H24:4/D256 noncausal observation
also retains its flag; fresh controls measured about 36.4-36.9 us. Raw attempts
and rechecks are preserved outside Git, without discarding unfavorable results.

Full BLAS validation is limited by existing Metal compilation failures:
simdgroup_matrix_storage address-space errors in GEMMKernel/AttentionKernel,
and get_destination_cooperative_tensor in quantized backward. The unchanged
baseline reproduced identical diagnostics; a full BLAS pass is not claimed.

Post-review cleanup normalizes the R1 causal pipeline key, restores explicit
16-byte threadgroup allocation alignment and preserves the original C++
conditional style. Descriptor selection requires all arguments explicitly.
The cooperative merge's 32-SIMD limit is documented. Cache-transition coverage
now uses an explicit large-V flag without ineffective specialization flags.
Its 68 FP16/BF16 causal/noncausal configurations include B1/R1 and B1/R2 at
C65537/D256, exercising >=512 partitions and the 32-SIMD, 1024-thread,
32-KiB reduction. All five decode tests and Metal API/shader validation passed.
Long-sequence scratch controls are recorded below.
Raw archives, hashes, harnesses and detailed logs are kept outside Git; REPRODUCE.txt identifies the evidence and permanent validation commands.

Batch-one long-sequence scratch controls, M5 Max, 2026-10-08
  FP16, R1, B1, causal; 10 configurations, two reversed passes, 64 complete
  selected-dispatch GPU samples and >=2 GPU seconds warmup per process.
  All 20 attempts had nominal thermal state and passed sampled CPU-reference
  and full finiteness checks. The temporary harness runs only the complete
  overridden dispatch, uses production private scratch allocation and batches
  eight warmup dispatches; recorded command buffers contain one dispatch.
  These are controlled partition overrides, not old-kernel comparisons.

  Production selects P512 at 64K and P1024 at 128K for both layouts here.
  At 64K, forcing P32 costs 34.1% (D128) or 41.4% (D256) more time while
  reducing scratch allocation from 16 MiB to 1 MiB. At D128, halving P costs
  18.4% at 64K and 17.4% at 128K. At D256, halving P costs 0.7% at 64K and
  is 0.5% faster at 128K; the two-pass result suggests a memory tradeoff for
  this layout, not a cross-device tuning rule. No selector change is made.
  With B1/R1, these layouts are capped at 32 MiB rounded scratch allocation
  once P reaches 1024. Longer sequences grow KV storage but not scratch.
  R2 doubles the requested partial-buffer size; larger head counts increase
  it proportionally. The small-buffer allocator retains the grown capacity.
  Post-cleanup checks: debug build and 5/5 decode integration tests pass;
  focused Metal API/shader validation passes both cache transitions and R2
  sinks. R1 causal/noncausal descriptor keys match in both precisions across
  10/40/80-core selections; R2 causal keys remain distinct.

At B1/R2/C262144, P=1024 for these layouts. D256/Hq16 requests 32.25 MiB,
D256/Hq24 requests 48.375 MiB and D128/Hq32 requests 32.5 MiB; all round
to 64 MiB. D128/Hq64 requests 65 MiB and rounds to 128 MiB. These are
partial-buffer requirements for this call; an already larger shared MFA
scratch buffer retains its existing capacity. Quantized attention has its
own scratch accounting and does not use this decode path.
